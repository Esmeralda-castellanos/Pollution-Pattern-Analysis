# Beneficiary & Impact Analysis — Pollution Pattern Analysis

## Primary Beneficiaries
- Municipal air-quality and environmental policy teams deciding where to focus mitigation
- Provincial environmental services planning monitoring network expansion
- GGD (public health services) assessing exposure burden by area
- Urban planners siting schools, housing, or care facilities relative to pollution profiles
- Residents in traffic-influenced or mixed-high-pollution cluster areas
- Environmental-justice and community advocacy groups

## Secondary Beneficiaries
- Dutch taxpayers, through more efficiently targeted monitoring/mitigation spend
- Other national air-quality networks that could reuse this profiling approach
- Researchers building on a validated station-profiling methodology

## Implementers
- RIVM and municipal environmental policy staff who would act on cluster-based recommendations
- Public-health officials incorporating exposure-profile insight into local health planning

## Impact Pathway
1. **Analytical outputs:** station cluster assignments, profile characterizations, cluster maps.
2. **Intermediate outcomes:** more targeted monitoring station siting; mitigation measures matched
   to a location's actual pollutant mix rather than a generic approach; increased stakeholder
   awareness of profile diversity hidden by national averages.
3. **Long-term impacts:** more equitable distribution of air-quality interventions; better-informed
   health-equity planning; a reusable methodology for future years or other monitoring networks.

## Equity Assessment
- **Potential harm/bias risk:** treating a cluster label as a fixed, permanent characteristic of a
  neighbourhood could unfairly stigmatize an area, or be used to justify inaction elsewhere
  ("that area is already fine"). Sparse station coverage means some areas are under-represented
  and could be mischaracterized by a nearby but non-representative station.
- **Promoting justice:** explicitly reporting cluster limitations (sparse coverage, single-year
  data, no causal claims) so results are used as a starting point for further investigation, not a
  final verdict on any location.
- **Power dynamics:** environmental agencies and municipalities hold decision-making power over how
  clusters translate into action; community groups should have a channel to question or contest a
  cluster-based prioritization affecting their area.
