# Ethics Framework — Pollution Pattern Analysis

## Stakeholder Impact Assessment
- Communities near stations placed in a "mixed high-pollution" cluster could face reputational or
  property-value effects if results are communicated without appropriate caveats.
- Areas without any nearby monitoring station are invisible to this analysis entirely — a form of
  exclusion baked into the data, not the method.
- Voices missing from the analysis design: residents of the areas being profiled were not
  consulted in choosing features or interpreting cluster meaning (acknowledged limitation of a
  capstone-scale project).

## Bias Audit
- Station placement itself may historically reflect where authorities expected pollution problems
  (e.g. near known industrial or traffic corridors) rather than a representative sample of all
  inhabited areas — meaning underserved areas without a station are systematically excluded, not
  just underrepresented.
- Cluster labels ("traffic-influenced," "background") will be interpreted using careful, ranged
  language (e.g. "relatively high NO2") rather than definitive categorical claims, to avoid
  overstating certainty for any single station.

## Consent & Participation Planning
- This capstone uses public, aggregated monitoring-network data (no individual-level or personal
  data), so individual consent/opt-out mechanisms are not applicable.
- If used in a real deployment, community review of cluster interpretations before publication
  would be a reasonable safeguard — noted here as a recommendation, not implemented in this
  capstone.

## Benefit-Risk Analysis
- **Benefits:** more targeted monitoring and mitigation; increased visibility for areas with a
  distinct, potentially under-addressed pollution profile.
- **Risks:** misinterpretation of clusters as proof of a pollution *source*; premature policy
  action based on a single year of data and sparse station coverage.
- **Judgment:** benefits justify proceeding, provided all outputs include explicit uncertainty and
  scope caveats (as specified throughout this project's instructions).

## Safeguards
1. No causal or source-attribution language anywhere in the notebook or reporting — clustering
   shows similarity, not cause.
2. Explicit "Evaluation and Limitations" section (Section 9 of the notebook) is mandatory, not
   optional.
3. Recommendations (Section 10) frame clusters as a starting point for further investigation
   (e.g. combined with traffic/emissions/land-use data), not a final determination.
